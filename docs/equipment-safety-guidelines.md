# Equipment Safety Guidelines

## General Rules

All personnel must complete a safety induction before operating any mining equipment. Equipment must be inspected at the start of every shift using the pre-operation checklist. Never operate machinery that has a red tag or a reported fault.

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is mandatory at all times on site:
- Hard hat (Class G minimum)
- Steel-capped boots (AS/NZS 2210.3)
- High-visibility vest
- Safety glasses
- Hearing protection in designated zones

## Heavy Machinery

### Excavators and Loaders

Operators must hold a current Certificate of Competency for the class of machine being used. Before moving the machine, sound the horn twice and wait three seconds. Maintain a 10-metre exclusion zone around operating heavy machinery. No personnel are permitted under a raised attachment unless it is mechanically locked out.

Refuelling must occur with the engine off. Do not refuel within 15 metres of a naked flame or ignition source.

### Haul Trucks

Maximum speed on haul roads is 30 km/h. On ramps the limit is 15 km/h. Trucks must yield to pedestrians at all marked crossings. Reversing requires a spotter when visibility is obstructed. Load limits are posted on each vehicle and must not be exceeded.

## Electrical Equipment

All electrical work must be performed by a licensed electrician. Isolate and lock out all energy sources before maintenance. Use a multi-lock station when more than one person is working on the same circuit. Test before touch — always verify isolation with a calibrated voltage tester.

Damaged cables, cracked insulation, or exposed conductors must be reported immediately and the equipment taken out of service.

## Reporting Faults

Report any equipment fault to your supervisor and log it in the Equipment Fault Register (EFR) in the site management system. Tag the equipment OUT OF SERVICE using a red tag. Do not remove another person's lock or tag under any circumstances.

Faults involving hydraulic leaks, brake failure, or steering issues are classified as critical and require immediate escalation to the Site Manager.
